Heal Social Foundation · Karachi, Pakistan

Research

The Research unit of Heal Social Foundation conducts quantitative community health research on the social, economic and environmental determinants of preventable illness in low-income urban populations in Karachi, and builds open data infrastructure for collecting and analysing community-level health data.

The unit works alongside the Foundation's four operating departments — Health, Education, Awareness and Love for Humanity — with a remit to ground each of them in community-level evidence rather than assumption. Its fieldwork is conducted through the Foundation's Community Public Health Initiative (CPHI).

Research question

Patients in Karachi's low-income neighbourhoods frequently reach government hospitals at a stage of illness at which treatment is difficult, expensive and less likely to succeed — for conditions that were, earlier in their course, preventable or straightforwardly treatable. The clinical burden is visible at the hospital. The processes that produced it are not.

The unit's programme asks what accounts for that interval. Specifically, it treats delay between symptom onset and presentation, and clinical severity at admission, as outcome measures, and examines how far they are predicted by household health literacy, care-seeking attitude, socioeconomic position, and environmental exposure — factors that, unlike the disease itself, are addressable through community-level work rather than clinical intervention.

The question is chosen because the Foundation is positioned to answer it. It operates in the neighbourhoods these patients come from and has established relationships in the hospitals they reach, which makes it possible to observe both ends of a pathway that is usually studied only at the hospital end.

Study area

The Foundation's operating catchment: Shah Faisal Colony, Korangi and Nath Khan Goth in Karachi, together with the surrounding katchi abadis, and patients treated at the National Institute of Child Health and other government hospitals serving these communities. The design also accounts for in-migration — a substantial share of patients presenting at Karachi's tertiary facilities travel from outside the city, and origin is recorded so that this can be distinguished from local burden.

Research infrastructure

Because no existing instrument captured the variables this question requires, the unit built one. Shaoor aur Shifa (شعور اور شفاء) is a self-contained platform for structured intake, storage and analysis of community health case data at participating sites.

Analytical functions

The platform implements the following over any filtered cohort:

  • Cohort filtering and cross-tabulation across eight dimensions — condition, town, facility, gender, age band, socioeconomic band, contributory exposure and attitude — with every view recomputing against the current subset
  • Bivariate analysis of health-literacy score against delay-to-presentation and against severity at admission
  • Age–sex pyramid and distribution analysis
  • Disease-burden decomposition by category and by preventability classification
  • Flow decomposition of contributory exposures through to outcome
  • Geographic aggregation and choropleth mapping by town, with median health literacy set against median household income to identify neighbourhoods where the two diverge
  • Facility catchment analysis, including the share of critical arrivals by hospital
  • Patient-origin flow analysis of in-migration to Karachi for tertiary care
  • Structured export of any filtered cohort, and a CSV intake template for participating sites

Architecture and data handling

The platform is a single self-contained HTML and JavaScript application. It has no server component, no database dependency and no external libraries, which means a participating site can run it offline on an ordinary machine, and the entire codebase can be read and audited by a small team. Data is held locally at the site and exchanged by structured CSV rather than through a central service.

Identity is excluded at the schema level rather than removed later: records carry an anonymised site code, and there is no field in which a national identity card number, name or address could be entered. Nothing at individual level is published.

Shaoor aur Shifa is a single-file application with no server component and no external dependencies, so that it can be audited by a small team and run offline in the field. Source code available on request.

Data status. Shaoor aur Shifa currently operates on a synthetic demonstration dataset of 520 generated records, produced by a deterministic pseudo-random generator embedded in the application. This dataset exists for interface development, analyst training and method testing only. It contains no real patient records and no data from any identifiable individual, and no result derived from it constitutes an empirical finding — the associations it displays are properties of the generator rather than observations. The platform is instrumentation built ahead of collection.

Field data collection

The unit has conducted one exercise in primary data collection: the CPHI Community Discovery Survey, a community survey administered on paper in the field and subsequently transcribed into a structured dataset of 58 variables across 33 completed forms.

The survey was designed to characterise the communities the Foundation works in ahead of any clinical data collection — establishing what households report about health access, cost, and care-seeking before the hospital-side instrument is deployed. The dataset is held in aggregate and is not published at individual level.

Ethics and data governance

Collection of identifiable patient case data has not commenced. Ethical review will be sought before any such collection begins.

Beneficiary data currently held by the Foundation is maintained under written consent obtained from beneficiaries or their guardians, in registers retained for regulatory inspection. The research instrument is designed so that identity is not collected in the first place rather than stripped afterwards, and no individual-level data from any source is published.
